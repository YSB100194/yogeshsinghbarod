Designing CDC for reliability

A decision-led framework for resilient change-data processing, replay safety, and trustworthy downstream consumption.

Disclosure boundary

This is a generalized, independently authored engineering reference. It is not a reproduction of any employer or client system, and it does not disclose workload counts, volumes, architecture, internal metrics, or confidential business outcomes. The patterns below are illustrative, not proof of a specific deployment.

The engineering problem

Incremental data movement must balance freshness, correctness, recovery, and operating cost. Events may arrive late, be duplicated, or require reprocessing after interruption.

Reference design

  1. Capture changes with stable event metadata.
  2. Persist to a durable transport or landing layer.
  3. Validate schema and ordering assumptions.
  4. Apply idempotent transformations.
  5. Publish freshness and data-quality signals.

Trade-offs to evaluate

  • At-least-once delivery versus exactly-once effects
  • Ordering guarantees versus parallel throughput
  • Replay windows versus storage and operational cost
  • Latency targets versus downstream consistency

How to measure outcomes

Define baselines for end-to-end freshness, recovery time, duplicate-handling correctness, exception rate, and on-call toil. Do not present hypothetical improvements as achieved results.

Engineering leadership perspective

The leadership task is to make failure modes visible, align owners on recovery expectations, and choose the right operational guarantees for the business—not merely optimize event throughput.
